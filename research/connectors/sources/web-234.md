The article maps four levels for connecting internal systems to Claude agents: Level 1 paste/upload static documents (no integration; stale context is failure mode #1); Level 2 native no-code connectors for Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 (permission-respecting real-time file search/read); Level 3 Zapier or Make.com no-code automation for tools like Salesforce, HubSpot, Jira, Notion, Airtable, and Slack (batch-style, seconds latency); and Level 4 custom MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers for real-time bidirectional access to internal APIs/databases, which require engineering. It claims Level 2 or Level 3 solve 80% of common needs, recommends choosing based on data change frequency (less than monthly → Level 1), Drive/SharePoint availability (→ Level 2), Zapier connector and batch use (→ Level 3), or proprietary real-time needs (→ Level 4), and warns against jumping straight to Level 4 or staying at Level 1 too long. A logistics example shows Level 2 or 3 usually solving customer-service needs, and the article suggests enabling a native connector as a 20-minute admin task to eliminate stale-context failures.
