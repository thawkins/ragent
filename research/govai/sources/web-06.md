Swapnil Gadkari argues architecture governance has shifted from waterfall-era bureaucratic oversight rooted in TOGAF and Zachman to a strategic enabler of “governed agility.” After agile and domain-driven design decentralized decisions in the early 2010s—causing fragmentation and technical debt, including one application rearchitected within 3 years—Gartner reports over 75% of enterprises are restarting or reinventing architecture programs. Modern governance embeds policy-as-code in CI/CD, uses federated models for microservices, platform engineering/IDPs and IaC, AI bill of materials (AI-BOM) and runtime behavioral policies for AI, shift-left security, and data mesh contracts. Recommended practices include automation-first compliance, domain ownership, strategic guardrails/ADRs, metrics such as deployment frequency, MTTR, and architecture fitness function compliance, plus psychological safety and shared responsibility.
