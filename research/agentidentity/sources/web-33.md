The Okta article argues that AI agent security must be identity-first: treat each autonomous agent as a non-human identity with a verifiable human owner, scoped permissions, just-in-time access, continuous monitoring, and lifecycle governance, using Zero Trust, fine-grained authorization (FGA), and frameworks like NIST AI RMF and OWASP LLM Top 10 to address prompt injection, data leakage, insecure tool use, supply chain risk, shadow AI/agent sprawl, and “Denial of Wallet.” It cites Bain’s 2025 executive AI survey, Gartner’s projection that 40% of enterprise applications will include task-specific AI agents by end-2026 (up from less than 5% in 2025), and Verizon’s 2025 DBIR finding that compromised credentials were the initial access vector in 22% of confirmed breaches. For compliance with HIPAA, GDPR/CCPA, SOC 2, and EU AI Act high-risk requirements (Arts. 12/14), it recommends tamper-resistant audit logs, HITL/HOTL oversight, secrets management, TLS 1.3, AES-256 via FIPS 140-3/CAVP-validated modules, data minimization, sandboxing/secure enclaves, anonymization/differential privacy/homomorphic encryption/MPC, and a kill-switch incident response. It concludes that a unified identity control plane for human and machine identities is foundational for accountable AI agent security at scale.
