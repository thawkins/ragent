```text
The Cloud Security Alliance AI Safety Initiative whitepaper (published 2026-05-20) argues that non-human identity (NHI) governance is the defining security gap of agentic AI, noting NHIs outnumber humans about 45:1 on average and 144:1 in cloud-native environments, only 15% of organizations are highly confident they can prevent NHI-based attacks, and over 16% do not track creation of AI-related identities. It cites rapid NHI and secrets growth and governance failures—44% NHI growth 2024–2025; 28.65 million hardcoded secrets added to public GitHub in 2025, including over 1.27 million AI-related secrets (+81% YoY); 47% of NHIs unchanged for over a year; 1 in 20 with full admin privileges; only 20% with formal API key offboarding—and incidents such as Salesloft-Drift (OAuth token reaching over 700 downstream customers), Moltbook (1.5 million API tokens exposed across 1.5 million agents), and OpenClaw prompt-injection exfiltration in 5 minutes. It identifies blind spots in ownership, lifecycle, privilege, inventory, compliance (SOC 2, ISO 27001, PCI DSS, NIST 800-53, EU AI Act), and shadow AI—IBM 2025 found 97% of AI-breach organizations lacked proper AI access controls, 20% of breaches involved shadow AI adding about $670,000, and 63% lacked AI governance policies. The whitepaper recommends a layered framework—centralized NHI registry, agent lifecycle management, zero standing privilege/JIT access, automated rotation and rapid revocation, third-party integration governance, and policy-led shadow AI management—supported by SPIFFE/SPIRE, ABAC/PBAC, secrets management, and behavioral monitoring; it notes the NHI access management market is projected to grow from $11.14 billion in 2025 to $27.33 billion by 2033.
```
