```text
Omada Identity’s blog argues that enterprises piloting AI agents face a governance gap because autonomous identities can interpret context, select tools, chain actions, and delegate across systems, potentially acting beyond intended authority even within existing permissions. It cites an August 2025 Brave demonstration in which an AI agent in Perplexity’s Comet browser was hijacked via indirect prompt injection—hidden webpage instructions made it follow the page instead of the user’s summarize request—and says identity governance cannot stop such injection but can stop the ensuing unauthorized action chain. Building on non-human identity governance, the post adds four dimensions—defined authority, delegation chain visibility, continuous runtime-drift checks, and decision evidence—citing alignment with the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, and OpenID Foundation’s 2025 agentic AI whitepaper, while noting fragmented platform models including Microsoft Entra Agent ID (preview), Salesforce Agentforce, ServiceNow AI Agent Studio, Amazon Bedrock, and Google Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform. It proposes a framework of accountability, defined authority, identity visibility, end-to-end reviewability, and traceable evidence, supported by a single accountable owner, a centralized control plane, and an escalation path.
```
