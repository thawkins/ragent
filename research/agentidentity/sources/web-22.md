```text
The article argues agentic AI shifts governance from model-centric to agent-centric and identity-centric, treating agents as first-class non-human identities requiring authentication, authorization, scoping, and audit across risks like unauthorized data access, privilege escalation via delegation chains, tool misuse, and prompt-injection policy bypass. It cites regulatory pressure for mandatory continuous logging: EU AI Act Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 Articles 12 (automatic event logging), 13 (transparency), 14 (human oversight/interruption), and 15 (accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity), plus NIST AI RMF's GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE functions and ISO/IEC 42001's AI management system, asserting "if you cannot show evidence, you cannot prove control." It recommends layered, correlated logging (using agent, session, request, tool-call, and policy-decision IDs) covering model/tool registry, perception events, plan and policy traces, tool-call execution, identity/access, human oversight, and outcome attribution, with tamper resistance, retention/minimization, and SIEM integration, alongside six policy domains: agent authorization/approval, data access boundaries, permissible actions, audit/logging, incident response/override, and lifecycle/decommissioning. It forecasts standardized audit schemas, formal non-human identity governance, contractual enforcement of AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001 controls, real-time oversight, and plan-level explainability.
```
