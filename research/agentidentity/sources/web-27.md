The article distinguishes non-human identities (NHIs) from AI agents: NHIs are machine/workload identities for APIs, service accounts, containers, workloads, and IoT devices, used for secure authentication and resource access, and are automation-first, system-integrated, high-volume, short-lived, predictable, and managed through credential lifecycle, access controls, and least privilege. AI agents are LLM-powered, autonomous, goal-directed, environmentally aware software systems that can initiate workflows, orchestrate APIs, update databases, manage schedules, and control devices; they lack identity, intent, or continuity of self, are not standalone identities but built on top of NHIs, and require behavior monitoring, guardrails, explainability, intent restriction, action-scope controls, verification, and observability. Treating them as the same is inaccurate and risky because NHI threats include exposed API keys and unused service accounts, while AI agent threats include autonomous overreach, overprivileged access, prompt injection, and misuse—two distinct threat surfaces needing distinct controls.
