On Feb. 17, NIST launched the AI Agent Standards Initiative, with an RFI on AI Agent Security due March 9 and a concept paper on Software and AI Agent Identity and Authorization due April 2, signaling that autonomous AI agents are entering federal governance and compliance. Run through CAISI, the initiative focuses on three pillars: industry-led standards and U.S. leadership in international bodies; community-led open-source interoperability protocols such as MCP; and research on agent security, identity, authorization, logging, revocation, and liability. It cites the November 2025 Amazon lawsuit against Perplexity over alleged User-Agent header violations and a Gravitee 2026 report finding only 14.4% of organizations launch AI agents with full security approval; NIST plans April listening sessions for healthcare, finance, and education, with sector guidance expected by year-end and regulatory incorporation in 2027. The article argues these voluntary standards will become de facto compliance obligations, urging organizations to respond to RFIs, inventory agents, update vendor contracts, and build governance now.
