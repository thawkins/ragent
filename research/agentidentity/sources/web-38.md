```text
Okta defines AI agent identity as an autonomous system’s unique, verifiable digital identity tied to scoped privileges, contextual awareness, and continuous lifecycle governance; unlike human or traditional service accounts, agents operate continuously at machine speed, maintain persistent context, and make dynamic, multi-step decisions requiring delegation chains, contextual authorization, and behavioral validation. Okta says organizations now manage at least 45 machine identities per human user, and AI agents magnify non-human identity risks—identity spoofing, stolen or long-lived credentials, malicious-input compromise, cascading tool and third-party API dependencies, and self-escalating permissions—while the EU AI Act’s human-oversight requirement for high-risk systems can conflict with autonomous operation. Recommended controls include unique agent identities, dynamic least-privilege authorization, behavioral analytics, secure and time-bound delegation, OAuth 2.0, managed identity services, encryption, human-in-the-loop checkpoints, red teaming, cross-functional governance, auditable decision-chain logging, immediate credential revocation, and future unified human/AI governance with quantum-resistant cryptography; Okta positions its platform for automated discovery, lifecycle management, and governance across cloud, SaaS, and hybrid environments.
```
