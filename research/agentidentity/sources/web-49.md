```text
B2B CIAM systems are adapting to manage non-human identities (NHIs) and AI agents, which operate without direct human oversight, often hold elevated privileges, and include service accounts, API keys, ML agents, and autonomous workloads—introducing lifecycle complexity and risks such as lateral movement, privilege escalation, credential compromise, and privilege misuse. Driven by AI model advances, standardized integration protocols, and efficiency competition, organizations require Zero Trust approaches including credential management, behavioral monitoring, and continuous verification; CIAM platforms must add automated credential discovery, ownership assignment, risk evaluation, dynamic client registration, pushed authorization requests, fraud-detection integration, agent-specific consent, and privacy-first token strategies. Curity’s Identity Server manages human and non-human identities with automated onboarding, access controls, lifecycle oversight, and standards such as OAuth2, OIDC, and dynamic client registration, while MCP highlights the need for identity-centric governance in AI-native ecosystems.
```
