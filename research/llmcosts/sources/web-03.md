Matlin et al. (Georgia Institute of Technology), in Findings of ACL 2025 (pp. 22633–22679), present FLAME (Finance Language Model Evaluation), claimed as the first holistic benchmarking suite for evaluating language models on core financial NLP (FinNLP) tasks. Following HELM's holistic criteria (standardization, recognition of incompleteness, multi-metric evaluation), the authors conduct the first comprehensive empirical comparison of standard and "reasoning-reinforced" LMs, evaluating 23 foundation models (e.g., GPT-4o, o1-mini, Gemini-1.5, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, DeepSeek-V3/R1, Llama 3, Qwen 2, Gemma 2, Mixtral) over 20 financial datasets across six task categories (text classification, information retrieval, summarization, sentiment analysis, question answering, causal analysis), all in zero-shot settings with open-sourced code, data, results, and a public leaderboard. Key findings: no single model dominates across tasks, though DeepSeek R1, OpenAI o1-mini, and Claude 3.5 Sonnet perform strongest overall; numeric reasoning is a major weakness (FNXL F1 scores often below 0.06) while summarization scores are relatively high (BERTScores ~0.75–0.82); and open-weight mid-scale models offer strong cost/performance trade-offs—inference over the full suite cost roughly $260 for DeepSeek R1 vs. $105 for Claude 3.5 Sonnet/o1-mini and $4 for Llama 3.1 8B. The paper also notes a scarcity of FinNLP datasets outside English and Chinese and offers a scenario-based taxonomy (tasks, domains, languages) to highlight data gaps.
