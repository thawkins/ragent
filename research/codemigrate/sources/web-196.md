In a Thoughtworks “Exploring Gen AI” article, Birgitta Böckeler describes an exploratory evaluation across 5 batches of small/medium/large greenfield business-logic tasks, using Sonnet 4.6 to generate solutions and judge TDD adherence and Opus 4.8 to blind-rank solution quality: she found no clearly discernible quality difference between TDD and non-TDD workflows, with Opus more than once ranking non-TDD solutions slightly higher on design/test quality and no meaningful mutation-score difference. Agents never followed TDD perfectly—often writing implementation first, skipping/faking the red step, or over-implementing—and TDD reliably cost several times more tokens (appendix: small 8.50x, medium 2.96x, large 4.89x), though cache/context effects overstate dollar cost. A key hypothesis was that non-TDD/test-first runs created a full design before coding, while TDD’s local minimal steps produced less coherent design; Böckeler says she has stopped telling coding agents to write tests first/TDD and instead focuses on mutation testing for regression quality, refactoring triggers, and Ivett Ördög’s Approved Scenarios.
