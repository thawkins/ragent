The 2026-07-31 AI-assisted codebase migration playbook argues that large LLM migrations depend on correct sequencing and reversibility, citing Bun’s 535,000-line Zig-to-Rust port in 11 days and Anthropic’s Mike Krieger’s 165,000-line Python-to-TypeScript move over a weekend. It prescribes building and debugging a behavioral oracle first (public-surface tests, golden outputs, or differential runs; Krieger used a 7-scenario parity harness and treated any behavioral change as a bug), then a rulebook/dependency map/gap inventory and 3-file dress rehearsal (Bun’s ~600-line PORTING.md took ~3 hours and caught two issues before 1,448 files), then verifiable-boundary queues delegated to disk state (Bun cleared ~1,600 compile errors in 12 hours). It recommends tracking judge pass rate, rule recidivism, diff review rate (often under 5% at million-line scale), rework per unit, and residual markers (Bun went 972→23→0 failing files), warns of silent semantic drift, hallucinated APIs in dynamic targets, and tests passing for wrong reasons, and concludes the real benefit is being able to delete the branch, fix the rules, and rerun—not speed.
