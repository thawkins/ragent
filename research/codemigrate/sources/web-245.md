```text
IBM’s article contrasts traditional AI, which automates well-defined application migration and modernization tasks such as discovery, dependency mapping, code conversion (COBOL, .NET, Java), testing, security/compliance scanning, observability, DevSecOps, and SDLC process definition, with agentic AI, which adds autonomy, context-awareness, adaptive decision-making, workflow orchestration, and human-agent squads across the AMM lifecycle. It describes three agentic roles—autonomous orchestrators, enablers, and reinvention catalysts—and IBM’s five-phase method (Initiation, Exploration, Conceptualization, Realization, Evolution), claiming benefits including speed/agility, accuracy/quality, better decision-making, resource optimization, risk reduction, continuous adaptation, and simplified processes while reducing WBS activities and keeping humans in the loop for novel solutions, authorization, and approvals. IBM positions its approach as using reusable assets, gen AI-powered agents, and agile squads to turn legacy systems into AI-native digital products, with governance and security needed to balance autonomy.
```
