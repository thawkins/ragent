In a Thoughtworks blog post, practitioners warn that AI-generated changesets are overwhelming code review, with larger changesets receiving quick “looks good to me” approvals that are then cited as proof of AI value. The post argues code review’s rigor—serving mentorship, consistency, correctness, and trust—must be deliberately redistributed to five areas: upstream spec/plan review; the test suite (e.g., reviewing 200 lines of tests instead of 10,000 lines of generated code, with TDD improving agent results); type systems (TypeScript as default, Rust as safer); risk maps (an 80/20 split of automated verification vs. deep human attention, plus heat maps of risk and change frequency); and continuous comprehension via ensemble programming and weekly architectural retrospectives. If not intentionally relocated, the rigor will simply evaporate even as faster merge times and bigger changesets look like progress.
