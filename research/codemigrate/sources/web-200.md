```text
AI-generated changesets are straining code review, and fast “looks good to me” approvals of larger changesets are being cited as proof of AI value; the article argues code review’s four functions—mentorship, consistency, correctness, and trust—do not disappear but must be deliberately redistributed. It identifies five migration areas: upstream spec/plan review; test suites/TDD (e.g., reviewing ~200 lines of tests instead of 10,000 lines of generated code, with TDD preventing agents from validating their own broken behavior); type systems (TypeScript for safety, Rust for stronger compiler constraints); risk maps using an 80/20 split of automated verification vs. deep human attention; and continuous comprehension through ensemble programming and weekly architectural retrospectives. It warns that unless rigor is intentionally redirected, it will dissipate even as faster merges and bigger changesets feel like progress.
```
