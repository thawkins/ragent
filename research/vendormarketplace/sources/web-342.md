By 2026, AI coding has shifted from autocomplete to delegation, with agents that plan goals, edit across dozens of files, run tests, and open PRs; the market splits into terminal/agentic CLIs (Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, and open-source OpenCode, Cline, Aider, Hermes), AI-native editors (Cursor, Windsurf), and IDE assistants (GitHub Copilot, Google Antigravity), with most production teams pairing an editor for line-level edits with an agent for goal-level delegation. Published starting tiers are ~$20/mo for Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex, $10–20/mo for Copilot, free preview for Antigravity, and token-only cost for open-source agents; benchmarks show Claude Opus 4.8 leading SWE-bench Verified at 88.6%, GPT-5.5 leading Terminal-Bench 2.0 at 82.7%, and Cursor Composer 2.5 offering top-three capability at 10–60× lower cost. The article concludes there is no single winner—Claude Code leads raw capability, Cursor wins flow/cost-efficiency, Codex anchors ChatGPT, and free/open-source options are viable—and recommends deterministic client-side tools for quick, privacy-sensitive tasks.
