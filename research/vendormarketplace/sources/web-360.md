Winder.AI’s comparison defines an agent harness as the code around a model—execution loop, tools, sandbox, memory, and context rules—distinct from frameworks that compose agents and platforms that run many harnesses over time. As of 19 August 2026 it compares nine harnesses—Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Qwen Code, DeepSeek Harness, Goose, Zed Agent, OpenHands, and Pydantic AI Harness—seven of which Winder runs in its own Helix fleet; DeepSeek’s developer-preview harness, shipped 13 August 2026, gained 95,386 GitHub stars in about two days. It argues harness choice can matter more than model choice, citing Vercel’s removal of 80% of tools raising success from 80% to 100% and cutting latency from 724s to 141s, LangChain’s Terminal Bench gain from 52.8% to 66.5%, CORE-Bench’s 42% vs 78% scaffold result, Harvey’s legal-agent accuracy more than doubling, and Letta Code’s 59.1% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 with Claude Opus 4.5 vs Claude Code’s 41.6%. The piece says to choose by assumptions about model lock-in, hosting, and who can change behavior rather than feature grids, notes convergence through the Agent Client Protocol and the Linux Foundation’s Agentic AI Foundation (MCP, AGENTS.md, Goose), and recommends harnesses when around ten Markdown-describable jobs exist, frameworks for deterministic branching, and an evaluation suite as the missing production requirement; Pydantic AI’s v2.0.0 (23 June 2026) is cited as harness-first with >50 capabilities.
