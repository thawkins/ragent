Scott Blackburn’s post warns that subsidized federal AI is ending: agencies obtained tools such as ChatGPT and Claude for as little as $1, but discounted pilots are converting to real market pricing through 2026–2027, and budgets are unprepared. Although AI running costs have fallen roughly 1,000x in three years, total bills are rising as usage—especially agentic AI—grows faster; analysts expect over 40% of agentic AI projects industry-wide to be canceled by 2027 due to runaway costs, with tight-budget, weak-control government programs most exposed. Blackburn and colleagues Aly Spencer, Gayatri Shenai, Davis Bailey, Elliot Mandel, and Thomas Lovatt Martin recommend treating AI as a distinct budget category with a named owner, paying for actual usage rather than blanket or seat licenses, tracking cost per outcome, using flexible contracts with exit clauses, and managing AI procurement as a continuous operating discipline tied to demonstrated value.
