```text
Zenskar argues pricing models must be judged by whether finance can bill and recognize revenue without spreadsheets, since model choices determine revenue predictability, who absorbs heavy-user costs, and recognition timing. It proposes a three-decision framework—what to charge for (access, seats, usage, credits, outcomes), how price scales (flat, graduated tiers, volume tiers, freemium), and contract structure (terms, ramps, milestones, commitments, bundles)—and notes that common labels like “tiered” and “usage-based” are not alternatives. Concrete examples include graduated tiers billing 1,500 units at $1,200 versus $900 under volume pricing, and volume pricing making 1,001 units cost $199.40 less than 1,000; under ASC 606, usage fees are recognized as usage occurs, prepaid credits sit in deferred revenue until consumed, and measured outcome-based savings are constrained variable consideration, while Intercom’s $0.99 per Fin outcome is a fixed fee known at delivery. It cites Kyle Poyar’s 2026 survey of 230 software and AI companies (median AI target gross margin ~50%; top 10% of power users account for >70% of token consumption) and reports hybrid pricing used by 37% of companies, up from 25%; iink Payments moved from a capped per-check fee to tiered subscriptions with usage overages, launched with zero engineering tickets, and shifted 15% of revenue to predictable subscriptions in its first full month.
```
