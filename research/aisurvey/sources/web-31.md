The article argues AI products should be priced around the customer’s job/value metric—such as resolved tickets, documents, or minutes—not tokens, with token cost as a floor and a target of at least 60% gross margin via hybrid subscriptions: included allowance plus overage, credits, or outcomes. It cites margin pressure—Bessemer found fast-growing AI companies averaged about 25% gross margin, and ICONIQ’s 2026 survey put average AI-product gross margin at 45% in 2025, projected to reach 53% in 2026—and Cursor’s June 2025 shift from a $20 Pro plan to “$20 of frontier model usage at API pricing,” which caused surprise bills, a July 4 apology, and refunds, though Cursor later said it passed $1B annualized revenue in November. ICONIQ reports AI companies now blend 1.7 pricing models on average, with consumption pricing rising from 35% to 42% and outcome pricing from 18% to 23%; the article’s worked example contrasts a $30 flat unlimited plan at 33% margin with a hybrid at 68% margin after AI costs, and warns that 2025–26 backlash came mainly from shrinking allowances without notice.
