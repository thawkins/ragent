The article compares seven 2026 AI-agent/LLM observability platforms—Laminar, Langfuse, LangSmith, Braintrust, Arize Phoenix/Arize AX, Datadog LLM Observability, and Opik—on agent-trace readability, instrumentation, OpenTelemetry support, traces-to-evals, human review, failure detection, cost attribution, self-hosting/licensing, and pricing; it is a vendor-authored documentation/pricing review (Laminar listed first), checked October 2026, not a benchmark. It argues agent observability must handle 1,000+-span runs, non-deterministic control flow, nested causality, and cross-invocation sessions, unlike single-call LLM tracing. Laminar is Apache 2.0 with transcript-first traces, Signals/clustering for recurring failures, SQL querying, and claimed ~20x storage reduction via deduplication (free 1 GB/7 days; Starter $30/month for 3 GB then $2/GB; Pro $150/month for 10 GB then $1.50/GB; self-hosting free), while Langfuse emphasizes prompt management (MIT except `ee/`, acquired by ClickHouse in January 2026), LangSmith is closed-source with LangChain/LangGraph integration and per-seat pricing, Braintrust is eval-first, Arize Phoenix is OTel/OpenInference-based under Elastic License 2.0, Datadog bills per LLM span with 40k free spans/month and APM integration, and Opik is Apache 2.0 and self-hostable.
