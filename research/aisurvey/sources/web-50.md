```text
Oxford Economics’ baseline sees generative AI making the US economy about 3.5% more productive over the next decade and 4.5% in the long run—toward the cautious end of published long-run estimates ranging from ~1% to over 12%—with clearer evidence so far in AI-related capex than realized productivity. Its Global Economic Model uses levers for AI exposure (clerical, administrative and IT jobs most exposed; mining, construction and fishing least; emerging markets less exposed than advanced knowledge-service economies), adoption (S-curve; US leading, Japan lagging; 80% of possible applications adopted by 2040 across most advanced economies), and labour augmentation versus replacement (baseline: mainly augmenting, without lasting unemployment). Scenarios show 2030s world GDP growth averaging close to 3% in AI Breakthrough versus 2.4% baseline, 2.8% in AI Disruption (similar productivity gains but higher structural unemployment), and around 2.2% in AI Disappointment (slower adoption, weaker productivity, weaker investment returns and equity/wealth/consumption effects).
```
