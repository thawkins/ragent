```text
The RBA’s October 2026 *Financial Stability Review* says the global financial system has weathered recent shocks with limited lasting disruption, supported by stronger post-GFC balance-sheet buffers and adaptable supply chains, but elevated geopolitical tensions, rapid AI/technology disruption, cyber/operational risk, and sovereign-bond-market vulnerabilities could threaten macro-financial stability and spill over to Australia. It highlights compressed equity and credit risk premia and growing AI exposure: technology-related firms accounted for nearly 30% of US investment-grade bond issuance in H1 2026; AI capital outlays could reach US$7.7 trillion by 2030; off-balance-sheet AI infrastructure obligations are estimated at US$1–1.5 trillion; and US hedge-fund repo borrowing exceeds US$3 trillion, close to 10% of US GDP. Private credit stress remains largely idiosyncratic but visible—US non-traded funds received over US$15 billion (around 12% of NAV) in redemption requests in Q2 2026, with about 60% unfulfilled—while bank exposures to private credit funds are less than 0.5% of bank assets in major economies. Large advanced-economy banks remain profitable and above regulatory capital and liquidity requirements, though persistent cost pressures could weigh on vulnerable borrowers.
```
