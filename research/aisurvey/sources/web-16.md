```text
Enterprise AI governance is defined as the policies, controls, and records determining who can use which AI tools and agents, on what data, at what cost, and how compliance is proven; the guide frames agent sprawl as the main 2026 problem, citing IBM’s 2025 finding that 63% of 600 organizations lacked AI governance policies and shadow AI added $670,000 to average breach cost, plus Microsoft/LinkedIn’s 2024 finding that 78% of AI users bring their own tools. It cites Gartner’s prediction that the average Fortune 500 enterprise will use over 150,000 agents by 2028, up from fewer than 15 in 2025, with only 13% believing they have adequate agent governance and over 40% of agentic AI projects canceled by end-2027 due to costs, unclear value, or inadequate risk controls. It maps NIST AI RMF 1.0, ISO/IEC 42001:2023, and the EU AI Act timeline—prohibitions and AI literacy from February 2025, GPAI rules from August 2025, transparency from August 2026, and Annex III high-risk rules from December 2027—and recommends builder/publishing rights, agent inventories with owners, permission inheritance, write-action approvals, audit trails, and cost-per-task measurement. Coworker is presented as an OM2 permission-aware context graph built from 50+ tools and exposed via MCP to Claude, ChatGPT, and Cursor, with SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, and CASA Tier 2, but no ISO/IEC 42001 certification.
```
