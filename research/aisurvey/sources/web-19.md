```text
At Snapdragon Summit 2026, Qualcomm demoed a 30B Mixture-of-Experts model running on end-point devices, with VP of AI Vinesh Sukumar arguing that "30B capacity ≠ 30B compute" because a router activates only the experts relevant to each token, cutting active compute, memory bandwidth, and power while enabling specialization across reasoning, coding, language, and domain tasks. The demo combines sparse MoE with UFS 5.0 and intelligent Flash-to-Memory expert management (Flash → DRAM → NPU memory → compute), letting a large expert pool persist without every expert residing in DRAM—positioning flash as a new tier of the AI memory hierarchy. Sukumar frames this as foundational for agentic AI workloads (reasoning, planning, tool use, multimodality, personalization) and predicts the edge race will be decided by sparse MoE, smarter memory hierarchies, and heterogeneous compute rather than larger dense models crammed into DRAM. Other posts on the page reference AMD's roughly $8B acquisition of World Labs, NVIDIA's open Nemotron 3.5 family (including the 120B Super VL with 12B active parameters), and AI infrastructure discussions at Assembling2026.
```
