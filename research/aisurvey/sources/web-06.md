Kong’s AI governance guide argues that enterprises need enablement—such as native MCP support for connecting agents to enterprise tools and data sources, plus SDKs and frameworks—alongside visibility, because “you can’t govern what you can’t see.” It cites AI spending reaching $2.59 trillion in 2026 and notes that Kong AI Gateway observes production traffic across infrastructure including compute, event streaming (Kafka, Kinesis, Pub/Sub, RabbitMQ), data stores, and hosted or self-hosted LLM endpoints. The core problem is a gap between compliance approvals and network reality: agentic AI governance often relies on manual reviews and “paper approvals,” leaving firewalls and gateways without context, while agents spread across clouds and model providers faster than governance programs can keep up.
