IBM argues enterprises should govern third-party AI agents by what they can do rather than who built them—assigning verified owners, limiting access and autonomy to business purpose, enforcing policy before consequential actions, and retaining evidence—while Gartner calls centralized agent management an emerging requirement and warns of execution risks, and NIST’s AI Agent Standards Initiative highlights identification, authorization, auditing, and non-repudiation. Recommended steps include inventorying every agent, classifying by autonomy/access/authority/impact, applying least privilege and “least agency,” enforcing rules as runtime controls, monitoring behavior, and preserving evidence; trust and authority should not automatically be transitive across delegated agents. IBM cites Riyadh Air, where IBM Consulting coordinated 59 workstreams across 60+ partners and watsonx Orchestrate coordinates agents across 75+ systems via 1,800+ integrations, and positions watsonx Orchestrate as an open agentic control plane with watsonx.governance and Enforcement Tracking.
