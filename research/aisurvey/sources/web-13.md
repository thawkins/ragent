The article argues that traditional IGA fails for AI agents because agents lack HR records, often have broad or ephemeral entitlements, and quarterly certification cannot evaluate request context; it cites Gartner’s 82:1 machine-to-human identity ratio and a contract assistant scenario where a broadly scoped SharePoint agent exposed a draft acquisition agreement despite having an owner and passing certification twice. It proposes five runtime-oriented controls: register every agent with an accountable human owner; approve data/tool scope by business category rather than role; bind each request to the human the agent serves via composite identity authorization; block unauthorized data at prompt and pre-retrieval stages; and govern scope changes, recertification, dormancy, and retirement continuously. It says IGA platforms should manage agents as identity objects, IdPs authenticate them, and a runtime authorization layer enforce decisions at access time, with audit evidence showing agent, user, data touched, policy version, and result for EU AI Act, SOX, or GLBA scrutiny. The article presents PlainID’s Runtime Authorization Platform as covering these controls via Full-Flow AI Guardrails, pre-retrieval filtering, LangChain/LangGraph authorization libraries, an MCP gateway, a SQL Authorizer, millisecond decisions, policy changes propagated in under 60 seconds, and an Authorization Graph; it also claims KuppingerCole Market Leader/Product Leader recognition, representation in Gartner’s Market Guide for Guardian Agents, 35 million identities secured, and billions of daily authorization decisions for Fortune 500 enterprises.
