Kore.ai’s practical guide argues that AI agent governance—organizational and technical structures for safe, accountable autonomous operation—must be enforced at runtime and measured by behavior: preventing violations, reconstructing decisions, and enabling intervention at machine speed rather than relying on periodic documentation. It identifies three interdependent pillars—AI risk management, trust/responsible AI, and compliance/regulatory alignment—and says legacy model-governance falls short because agents act autonomously, evolve, interact in multi-agent systems, and outpace human review. The guide cites regulatory pressure including the EU AI Act’s high-risk requirements and penalties of €35 million or 7% of global revenue, plus emerging US state rules in California, New York, and Colorado; it also claims proactive governance speeds deployment, with the tenth agent deploying 3–5x faster by inheriting proven infrastructure.
