The article argues that AI agents’ rapid rise—computer-task accuracy rose from 12% to 66.3% in 2025, just six points below the human baseline, and Precedence Research projects the market growing from $8 billion in 2025 to $199 billion by 2034—requires governance to shift from static model oversight to dynamic system governance focused on what agents did, why they were allowed to act, and who is accountable. A May 2026 World Economic Forum panel at AI+Expo in Washington, D.C., emphasized that risk-based, life-cycle, accountability, safety, and human-oversight approaches must become far more operational as agents act faster with less human intervention; the article cites uses such as manufacturing agents diagnosing and removing defects and the CDC’s estimated 527% return on its staffwide AI chatbot, while warning about auditability, prompt injection, accountability gaps, and uneven global capacity. It recommends explicit permissions and access controls, stronger deployer-developer feedback loops, better agent benchmarks and evaluations, incentives for testing and assurance, and international interoperability with local adaptation, citing frameworks including the WEF’s Agent Capability and Authorization Profile, NIST’s AI RMF, the EU AI Act—whose high-risk obligations are deferred to December 2027 and August 2028—ISO/IEC 42001, and Singapore’s January 2026 Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI.
