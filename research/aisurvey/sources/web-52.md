At an IMF-World Bank side event in Bangkok on Oct. 17, 2026, Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark said AI could expand U.S. GDP to about $44 trillion by 2030—nearly 50% above the ~$30 trillion current level, per Reuters—but that figure is Anthropic’s “Extreme” scenario; its Sept. 2026 models estimate 2030 real GDP in 2025 prices at $34.1T (Modest), $36.3T (Substantial), and $44.4T (Extreme), versus ~$33.6T without AI, with survey-based expectations implying lower growth of 2.4%, 5.4%, and 15.4%. The article then highlights financing risks: BIS Bulletin No. 120 (Jan. 7, 2026) says AI capex will increasingly rely on debt and private credit, while CoreWeave bond yields rose to ~15%, Oracle-linked “Project Jupiter” saw ~$18B in loans stall at 89–91 cents on the dollar and a force-majeure power delay of about a year, and 2031 bond spreads ranged from 41bp for Alphabet to 227bp for Oracle and 970bp for CoreWeave. It also cites the IMF’s Oct. 13 Global Financial Stability Report, which flagged 14 AI-related transactions since 2023 involving ~$300B equity, ~$650B debt, and ~$1.4T future revenue contracts and “wrong-way risk,” while CFTC data as of Oct. 13 showed leveraged funds net short 33,673 yen futures and 14,941 Swiss franc futures. Upcoming earnings—Meta on Oct. 21, Microsoft and Alphabet on Oct. 28, and Amazon on Oct. 29—are presented as key tests, alongside the question of whether credit stress remains limited to highly leveraged AI-infrastructure firms or spreads more broadly.
