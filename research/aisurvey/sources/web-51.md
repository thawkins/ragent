The article argues that AI labs such as Mistral are vertically integrating into compute—Mistral raised $830M in bank financing in March 2026 to buy 13,800 Nvidia chips for a Paris-region data center and later secured a multibillion-dollar capacity deal with Microsoft—but infrastructure ownership exposes them to pricing, utilization, operating-cost, GPU-obsolescence, and debt risks. Demand is tight (CBRE: 1.4% vacancy and 80.4% pre-leasing across eight major North American data-center markets in H1 2026; IEA: about 20% of planned data centers could face delays from grid constraints, with new transmission lines taking 4–8 years), yet Nvidia’s FY2026 $215.9B revenue and 71.1% gross margin do not prove downstream profitability. CoreWeave illustrates the tension: Q2 2026 revenue of $2.58B and backlog of ~$104B, adjusted EBITDA of $1.51B (59%), but an operating loss of $49M, net loss of $626M, net interest expense of ~$640M, H1 capex of $14.12B versus $3.66B operating cash flow, ~$15B in principal due from H2 2026 through 2028, and 62% of Q2 revenue from two customers. The article also flags circular financing—Nvidia’s $6.3B capacity-purchase agreement with CoreWeave through April 2032 and equity stake, plus BIS’s finding that 55.2% of 2021–2025 AI investment value came from other AI businesses—and warns that AI can keep growing while debt-financed infrastructure operators and their investors suffer losses.
