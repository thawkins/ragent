A 2026 open-access review in *Discover Informatics* by Azhar A. Hadi and K. P. Supreethi (JNTU Hyderabad) screened more than 400 papers down to 120 studies and maps 13 pretrained multimodal models released 2020–2025 across 11 application domains. It traces architectures including ViT’s 16×16-pixel patches, CLIP’s 400M image-text pairs, BLIP/BLIP-2’s frozen encoders and Querying Transformer, Flamingo’s Perceiver Resampler and gated cross-attention, LLaVA’s GPT-4-synthesized visual instruction tuning, GPT-4V, GPT-4o (May 2024; first LLM to perform real-time video emotion recognition), Florence-2 (FLD-5B: 5.4B annotations across 126M images), SigLIP-2 (sigmoid loss; up to 1M-example batches; 100+ languages), PaliGemma 2 (3B–28B parameters), Gemma 3 (1,024-token sliding window; 128K context), and Llama 4 (sparse Mixture-of-Experts; ~10M-token context). Reported results include 98.5% lung-disease accuracy, 90.32% tuberculosis chest-X-ray accuracy with PaliGemma-CXR, 93% IoU tooth segmentation with ChatIOS, 97.66% license-plate character accuracy with fine-tuned PaliGemma versus 67% for GPT-4o on pedestrian behavior prediction, and 99.9% AUROC with 6.6 ms inference for CLIP-based defect classification, alongside GPT-4V’s 87.1% anatomy versus 35.2% pathology accuracy and >40% diagnostic hallucination rates in some evaluations. The review flags small/single-center/noisy datasets, 120B-parameter compute costs, hallucination and adversarial vulnerability, and bias/privacy risks, and calls for multi-center annotated datasets, encoder refinements for long audio and high-resolution images, memory-efficient transformers, explainability as core evaluation, and distillation/Mixture-of-Experts scaling (DOI: 10.1007/s44564-026-00022-1).
