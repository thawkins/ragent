A fourth annual enterprise AI study by Wharton Human-AI Research and GBK Collective, published 13 October and surveying 1,416 U.S. enterprise leaders plus entry- and mid-level employees, found that 80% of leaders report positive AI returns but only 28% report strongly positive returns. Strong ROI was associated with measuring growth outcomes (35% vs. 13% for efficiency alone), having a newly designated Chief AI Officer (58% vs. 24% where oversight was added to an existing executive and 8% where no role exists or is planned), standardizing on one approved AI platform (50% vs. 9% for undefined experimentation), and clearing workflow-transformation measures (67% vs. 28% overall); only about one in five organizations meets all three workflow measures, and only 7% prove value, transform workflows, and align the organization. Strong-ROI organizations were no more likely to report staff reductions or hiring freezes, while 52% of leaders said AI’s primary job over the next 12–24 months is productivity or cost reduction versus 22% naming growth; 67% use agents in some form, but only 28% say AI is fully embedded in core processes and 17% let agents act without per-step approval, and perception gaps exist between leaders and employees on workflow change (73% vs. 58%) and adequate AI training (78% vs. 56%).
