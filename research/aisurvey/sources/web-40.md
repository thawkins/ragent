```text
AI token economics is the practice of measuring, allocating, optimizing, and governing the cost and value of tokens consumed by AI applications and agents, aiming for **cost per valuable outcome** rather than lowest cost per token. Tokens are metered as input, output, reasoning, cached, and multimodal units; output tokens commonly cost 4–6× input, reasoning tokens are billed at output rates but hidden, and model-specific tokenizers make cross-provider comparisons difficult. In June 2026 the Linux Foundation announced intent to launch a **Tokenomics Foundation**—with founding members including Oracle, Salesforce, and IBM, and nOps as a Premier Member—signaling standardization as falling unit prices fail to reduce bills: AT&T scaled from ~8B to 27B tokens/day, Google reported ~1.3 quadrillion tokens/month, and the IEA said AI data-center electricity demand grew ~50% in 2025 versus ~3% globally, while reasoning/agentic workloads use 5–30× more tokens per task and agentic tasks can span ~15,000 to >1M tokens versus 200–2,000 for chat. Enterprises spent ~$37B on generative AI in 2025; optimization levers include model routing (up to 98% cost reduction in published benchmarking), sentence-level RAG filtering (70–80% fewer tokens), prompt caching, batching, and controlling agent loops/retries, with usage attributed by provider, model, application, feature, team, customer, environment, workflow, and user.
```
