In a LinkedIn post, Michelle Leeuwon argues that AI is making invention easier but may make commercialization harder. At a Gulf Coast Consortia technology transfer lunch, she discussed building startups by combining complementary technologies rather than relying on a single invention, noting that AI can accelerate technology assessments, competitive landscape analysis, market research, IP reviews, and agreement drafting, while faster and cheaper development—especially for AI software—may shorten the commercial value of individual inventions. She says the key question is shifting from “Is this technology patentable?” to “What makes this opportunity commercially defensible, and what will it take to bring it to market?”, with value potentially residing in proprietary data, specialized know-how, technical validation, complementary technologies, regulatory pathways, industry relationships, or execution capabilities; licensing professionals remain essential to turn information into strategy, relationships, decisions, and commercial outcomes.
