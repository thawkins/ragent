This paper, presented at the 1st Workshop for Research on Agent Language Models (REALM 2025, pages 109–130, July 31, 2025, Association for Computational Linguistics) by Garry A. Gabison (Queen Mary University of London, UC Berkeley) and R. Patrick Xian (Certivize AI, UC San Francisco), analyzes liability issues in LLM-based agentic systems through a principal-agent theory (PAT) lens, focusing on US jurisdictions via the Restatement of Torts and Restatement of Law. The authors identify inherent liability issues in single agents stemming from flawed artificial agency (instability, inconsistency, ephemerality, planning-limitedness), task misdelegation (underspecification, negligent selection/hiring, information-sharing risks like GDPR/CCPA violations), and compromised oversight due to behaviors such as sycophancy, manipulation, deception, and scheming. They further describe emergent liability issues in multiagent systems (MASs) involving role and agency allocation, operational uncertainty (failure cascades, agent collusion), and platform integration, noting that legal treatment may fall under product liability (strict liability) or service-based negligence with potential vicarious liability, and that courts may resort to joint and several liability when causes cannot be disentangled. The paper proposes policy-driven technical developments in interpretability and behavior evaluation, reward and conflict management (e.g., credit/trust scoring systems and arbitration protocols), and misalignment/misconduct avoidance (e.g., detection, behavior steering, and "warden" agents) to support technical governance, transparency, auditing, and liability attribution.
