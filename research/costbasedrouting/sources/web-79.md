  One anti-pattern I keep seeing (again and again) is teams falling in love with a shiny new technology… and then going
  hunting for a problem it can solve. We saw it with cloud. Then again with microservices. And now we’re seeing it with
  AI. There’s nothing wrong with modernization — in fact, it’s often necessary. But modernization should be driven by
  real business needs and real engineering constraints, not by hype or fear of being left behind. It’s easy to get swept
  up in the wave of whatever the latest “must-have” thing is. But technology is a tool, not a strategy. One approach
  I’ve found helpful to counter this is using industry tech radars and building an internal tech radar for your
  organization. It creates shared visibility into: what the industry thinks about emerging tools what your teams are
  experimenting with what’s approved, discouraged, or still under evaluation More importantly, it helps shift the
  conversation from “How can we use this?” to “Do we actually need this?” Here’s a great example from
  [Thoughtworks][102]: [https://lnkd.in/gEvvkaqj][103] They have a nice tool to help you create your own tech radar on
  that same page.
