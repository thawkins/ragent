Earlier this week, we launched Extended Prompt Caching for Azure OpenAI models in Microsoft Foundry 🚀 By extending
cache retention from minutes to up to 24 hours, customers can achieve higher cache hit rates for their long-context,
multi-turn, and iterative AI workloads… improving cost efficiency 💰, reducing latency ⚡, and increasing throughput 📈.
A big step forward for more efficient inference at scale. Learn more: [https://lnkd.in/gtN3vwZC][15]

  🚀 **Reacting to this timely piece from the Google Cloud team on [Dev.to][100]:** *“Surviving the eviction: How to
  build interrupt-resilient AI workloads on GKE”* 👉 [Link to article]([https://lnkd.in/giSGVhSN][101]) In the rush to
  deploy large AI models, **preemptible VMs and spot instances** are often hailed as cost-saving heroes. But as the
  article reminds us, they come with a catch: **interruptions**. If your training job gets evicted mid-epoch, you’re not
  just losing compute – you’re losing days of work and risking model drift. The piece covers pragmatic patterns for GKE:
  checkpointing, multi-region pooling, and graceful shutdown handlers. My take? This isn’t just about Kubernetes configs
  – it’s a **cloud health mindset**. Interrupt-resilience forces you to design for failure, which is exactly what
  production AI needs. 💡 **Key insight:** The most cost-effective AI pipeline isn’t the one with the cheapest nodes –
  it’s the one that can survive being turned off and pick up where it left off without manual babysitting. At **Nuance
  AI & Cloud Health Insights**, we see enterprises increasingly balancing cost efficiency with reliability. This article
  is a solid starting point for teams moving from “we can run it on spare capacity” to “we can run it reliably on spot.”
  **Question for the community:** Are you using spot/preemptible instances for AI workloads? What’s your biggest pain

  wins ✍ Real takeaway: The "Azure OpenAI is more or less secure" narrative is outdated. 🤔 The real question is: where
