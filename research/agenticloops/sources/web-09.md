This article in *Construction Robotics* (2025, 9:1, published online December 17, 2024) by Yuning Wu, Emek Erdolu, Jiaying Wei, Jean Oh, and Daniel Cardoso Llach of Carnegie Mellon University presents a human-centered design and evaluation of a mobile robot to assist carpentry workers installing formwork panels on a U.S. building site. Based on a 15-week ethnographic study—seven weeks of on-site observation plus eight weeks of interviews with nine workers—on an 18,700 sq ft construction floor hosting 20–35 workers at a time, the team identified tool and hardware delivery as an ancillary task suited to robotic support, noting one worker made 19 such delivery trips within an hour. The prototype, built on a Clearpath Husky platform with 3D/2D LiDAR, RGB cameras, a retractable tray (carrying items like an impact wrench, rotary drill, angle grinder, clamps, and bolts), and an innovative deep reinforcement learning (DRL) architecture for social navigation, supports three commands—send, summon, and accompany—and was evaluated in simulations, lab tests, and on a construction floor, demonstrating apt social navigation and improved worker comfort relative to conventional approaches. The authors propose "robot in the loop" as a design pattern combining ethnographic and technical design research, and introduce a new human-centered benchmarking metric emphasizing worker comfort and navigation efficacy, arguing that no prior construction robotics work has applied such ethnographic methods or RL-based social navigation in live construction contexts.
