# Summary

This Scientific Reports (2023, 13:603) survey by Muzahid et al. examines multiple vehicle cooperation and collision avoidance (MVCCA) in automated vehicles (AVs), noting that AV systems are especially prone to rear-end collisions—which account for 42.7% of all accidents and typically trigger chain/multiple-vehicle collisions (MVCs), which represent ~20% of all traffic collisions, 18% of US motorway deaths, and up to 50% of urban traffic congestion. Motivated by the statistic that 94% of road accidents stem primarily from driver error (e.g., distraction, poor visibility, excessive speed), the authors review existing literature—mostly limited to single-vehicle collision avoidance (SVCA)—and construct a taxonomy covering five AV aspects: perception (environment fusion, sensor fusion), communication (V2V/V2X), threat assessment, decision-making, and vehicle control. The paper identifies key challenges including mixed traffic management (AVs coexisting with conventional vehicles), cooperative maneuver scalability, multi-agent robotics systems, insufficient training data (summarizing datasets like Apollo, Oxford RobotCar, Argoverse, JAAD), and simulator limitations (SUMO, MATSim, AIMSUN, PTV Vissim, OMNeT++, NS-3). It proposes an AI-enabled conceptual framework and a deep reinforcement learning-based decision-making model for multi-agent traffic environments, discusses four-phase MVC evolution (regular driving, pre-crash/point-of-no-return, first crash, induced multiple collisions), describes critical scenarios (highway sudden slowdown/blockage; sudden lane change), and notes typical driver reaction times of 0.75–1.5 seconds, aiming to guide future implementation of driverless automated traffic systems.
