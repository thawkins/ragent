Romero, Zimmerman, Steinfeld, and Tomasic (Carnegie Mellon University), in an AAAI Fall Symposium Series (FSS-23) paper, explore integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) with Cognitive Architectures (CAs) for more robust AI, noting that LLMs (e.g., ChatGPT, GPT-4, PaLM 2) suffer from interpretability, consistency, hallucination, and bias issues while CAs (e.g., ACT-R, SOAR, CLARION, LIDA) struggle with knowledge representation and scalability. They propose three integration approaches—each with preliminary empirical evidence and illustrated by a cognitive agent for assisting visually impaired users with indoor navigation and public transit: a modular approach (four cases of varying integration degrees, using chain-of-thought prompting and drawing on augmented LLMs, the Common Model of Cognition, and the simulation theory of cognition), an agency approach (inspired by Society of Mind and LIDA, with specialized agents competing and forming coalitions at micro and macro cognitive levels), and a neuro-symbolic approach (inspired by CLARION's action-centered subsystem, where LLMs occupy the connectionist bottom level and bottom-up learning extracts symbolic rules—Independent-Rule-Learning and Rule-Extraction-Refinement—while top-down guidance directs prompt engineering). Common findings across approaches include the value of working memory over LLM context-window truncation, LLMs' ability to translate natural language to symbols and vice versa, benefits of multi-modal multi-turn interaction for building shared mental models, and shared susceptibility to LLM stochasticity; the paper also discusses tradeoffs and challenges of each approach.
