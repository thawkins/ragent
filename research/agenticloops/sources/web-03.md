This is a survey/review article published in *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* (2024, 11:1259) by Gao, Lan, Li, Yuan, Ding, Zhou, Xu, and Li of Tsinghua University (BNRist and Department of Electronic Engineering), examining the integration of large language models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT, Gemini, LLaMA, Alpaca, and GLM into agent-based modeling and simulation (ABMS). The authors argue that LLM agents overcome key limitations of traditional approaches (predefined rules, symbolic equations, stochastic models, and machine learning) by providing autonomy, social ability, reactivity, and pro-activeness, enabling human-like perception, reasoning/decision-making, adaptive learning and evolution, and heterogeneity/personalization. The survey identifies four major challenges—environment perception, human alignment, action generation, and evaluation—and systematically reviews recent works organized into four domains (cyber, physical, social, and hybrid), covering both real-world and virtual environments, using a PRISMA-based literature selection process (filtering out 20+ papers that used LLM agents merely as assistants rather than for ABMS). It concludes by proposing future research directions including scaling up simulations, open simulation platforms, robustness, and ethical risks, and maintains a repository of representative papers at github.com/tsinghua-fib-lab/LLM-Agent-Based-Modeling-and-Simulation.
