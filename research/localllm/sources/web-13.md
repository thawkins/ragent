```text
Apple has published new details on its MLX machine learning framework and the Neural Accelerator integrated into its M5 processor, aimed at users running local AI workloads such as large language models (LLMs) on Apple Silicon. Demonstrated configurations range from a Mac Studio cluster of four 512 GB RAM machines networked via Thunderbolt 5 running the Kimi K2 Thinking model, to a MacBook Pro M3 Max with 128 GB RAM running OpenAI's gpt-oss-120b, with MLX-optimized model variants providing additional performance gains. The macOS 26.2 Beta adds support for latency-free Thunderbolt 5 networking and the M5's neural accelerators, which speed up ML inference; however, because no M5 Pro, Max, or Ultra machines exist yet and the base M5 supports only up to 32 GB RAM, Apple notes M4 Max or M3 Ultra systems may currently be better choices. Apple claims models that fit in RAM show a 3.3× (gpt-oss-20b-MXFP4-Q4) to 4× (Qwen3-8B-MLX-4bit) faster "Time to First Token," and points developers to the MLX-LM project on GitHub, the MLX community on Hugging Face, and tools like LM Studio for accessing MLX model variants.
```
