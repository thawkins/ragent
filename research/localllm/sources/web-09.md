This AIMultiple article (retrieved September 17, 2026) compares NVIDIA DGX Spark inference benchmarks against alternatives including RTX workstations, AMD Ryzen AI Halo, Framework Desktop, Mac Studio, and GB10 OEM systems (ASUS Ascent GX10, Dell, Acer, GIGABYTE). In LMSYS spreadsheet data using GPT-OSS 20B MXFP4 in Ollama at batch 1, the RTX 5090 generated tokens 3.4× faster than Spark (205.5 vs. 60.9 tokens/s), with the RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell at 215.2 and RTX 5080 at 140.9 tokens/s, while Spark (60.9) beat Mac Studio M1 Max (52.7) and Mac mini M4 Pro (46.9); however, the sheet lacks prompt lengths and runtime versions, and the review body cites a conflicting Spark figure of 49.7 tokens/s. February 2026 llama.cpp tests showed Spark's GPT-OSS 120B decode falling 27% (58.72 → 42.76 tokens/s) at 32,768 tokens of prior context, with prefill at 2,443.91 tokens/s for a 2,048-token prompt. AMD's vendor-reported May 2026 tests claim its Ryzen AI Halo (Ryzen AI Max+ 395, 128 GB) beats Spark by 4–14% across four models, though absolute rates were undisclosed; pricing cited was $3,999 for Halo vs. $4,699 for Spark, and Framework's 128 GB configuration lists at $3,449. Apple announced M5 Max/Ultra Mac Studios on August 25, 2026 (starting $2,499/$5,499, up to 128 GB/512 GB and 1.2 TB/s bandwidth), but no reproducible M5-versus-Spark benchmark exists. The article stresses that the three sources do not form a single controlled ranking: Spark offers 128 GB unified memory at 273 GB/s versus RTX 5090's 32 GB GDDR7, so model fit, memory capacity, and software stack—not a single speed figure—should drive selection.
