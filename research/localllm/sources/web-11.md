```text
GMKtec claims its EVO-X2 mini-PC, powered by AMD's Strix Halo APU (Ryzen AI Max+ 395), rivals NVIDIA's $4,000 DGX Spark (GB10 chip) in on-device AI inference at nearly half the price—$2,199 (actual MSRP $2,800) for a 128 GB + 2 TB configuration, with some DGX Spark models near $3,000. In GMKtec's internal testing across Llama 3.3 70B, Qwen3 Coder, GPT-OSS 20B, and Qwen3 0.6B, the EVO-X2 led in token generation on Llama 3.3 70B (4.9 vs. 4.67 tok/s) and GPT-OSS 20B (64.69 vs. 60.33 tok/s), while NVIDIA won on Qwen3 Coder (38.03 vs. 35.13) and Qwen3 0.6B (174.29 vs. 163.78); AMD won first-token response time in three of four tests (e.g., GPT-OSS 20B: 0.19s vs. 0.44s), attributed to its CPU+GPU+NPU layout and XDNA 2 engine reducing latency. The article concludes the DGX Spark remains better for high-throughput large-model workloads (the GB10 promises 1 PFLOP at FP4), while the Strix Halo platform offers similar latency-sensitive, real-time inference performance at much lower cost.
```
